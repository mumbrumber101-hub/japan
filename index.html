R. A. Lafferty

Reading Lafferty
in Japanese

An annotated guide to criticism, reviews, translation, and fandom

Sources checked September 16, 2026

Japanese readers have been writing about Lafferty for decades. Alongside book reviews are translators’ recollections, convention reports, and detailed accounts of novels read in English. Some writers examine the cruelty, sorrow, and religious belief within his comedy; others describe the pleasure or frustration of reading him. Collected here are links to their writing, with English descriptions.

In a 2002 memorial discussion, Matsuzaki reported a small reader survey in which favorites were scattered across more than forty stories. The discussion also considers communication, perception, and loneliness, and asks how adequately the usual descriptions of Lafferty as a science-fiction writer or teller of tall tales account for his work. Memorial discussion, 2002.

At the 2012 Kyoto SF Festival, participants discussed how American editors and Japanese translators had shaped the Lafferty known in Japan. They questioned his reputation as a drunken comic storyteller and stressed his learning and religious convictions. The choice of stories for translation, they suggested, had helped determine how readers understood him. Kyoto SF Festival report, 2012.

Ren Hanna makes a similar complaint in his 2021 essay. He argues that the familiar description of humorous tall tales obscures the cruelty, terror, and sadness of the stories, and proposes an introductory collection that would give those qualities their due. Other writers compare Lafferty with Chesterton, Latin American fiction, Ultraman, and manga. Several trace his influence on contemporary Japanese SF. Ren Hanna, “A Phantom Story Collection”.

The 228 entries are arranged by subject and principal work, then by date. Japanese article titles appear with English translations. English titles supplied for Japanese collections are descriptive unless an English original is identified. Dates distinguish events from later reports or reprints where possible. Incomplete and unavailable sources are marked.
